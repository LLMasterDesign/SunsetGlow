## Fixed Cost Allocation Rules

### 1. Takedown (7.5%)
- **Purpose:** End-of-season removal, careful storage, organization for next year
- **Why it's worth it:** Faster installs next season = lower labor hours = higher throughput
- **Question to reduce:** Could we batch takedowns by neighborhood to reduce drive time? Could we train customers to self-remove (with discount)?

### 2. Marketing / Flex Budget (5%)
- **Purpose:** Dual-use discretionary fund
  - Sales rep commission (when applicable)
  - Customer loyalty credits (add-ons, discounts, referral gifts)
  - Marketing spend (ads, promos, referral bonuses)
- **Key:** Doesn't always have to be spent; can roll to profit if not needed
- **Tracking:** CRM or simple spreadsheet to track credits per customer
- **On paper:** Shows as allocated expense for accounting/investor clarity, but flexible in practice

### 3. Labor (32.5%, Fixed)
- **Protected:** Does NOT get reduced by discounts
- **Crew structure:** 2-3 installers per job
- **Install time:** 3.5-4.5 hours for a $1,200 job
- **Pay model:** $15/hr base + performance-based commission from the 32.5% labor pool

#### Pay Structure (% of 32.5% labor allocation)
| Role | % of Labor Pool | $ on $1,200 job | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Novice** | 20% of 32.5% | $78 | ($390 × 20% = $78) |
| **Experienced** | 25-30% | $98-$117 | Scales with skill |
| **Team Lead** | 35% + 5% truck | $156 | ($390 × 40% = $156) |

**Example 2-person crew on $1,200 job:**
- Team Lead (40%): $156
- Novice (20%): $78
- **Total paid:** $234 (60% of $390 labor pool)
- **Remaining 40%:** $156 (covers payroll taxes, insurance, buffer)

**Retention strategy:**
- Raise reviews every 20-30 homes or 2 weeks
- % allocation increases with performance (20% → 25% → 30% → 35%)
- Keeps top talent engaged and paid competitively

## Discounting Strategy (Seasonality)

### Early Season (Sept–Oct): Discounts to drive volume
- **Typical discount:** 10-20% off
- **Where it comes from:** The $660 remainder (after takedown, commission, labor)
- **Example:** 20% discount on $1,200 job = $240 discount
  - Remaining margin pool: $660 - $240 = $420 to cover COGS + profit

### Peak Season (Nov–Dec): Upcharge or hold price
- **Strategy:** No discount, or small premium (5-10%) for rush jobs
- **Why:** High demand, limited slots
- **Example:** 10% premium on $1,200 = $1,320 total
  - Margin pool increases: $660 → $726

### Rule: Labor = Sacred (32.5% min)
- **Max discount without hurting labor:** Discount can't push total revenue below labor coverage
- **Safe discount ceiling:** ~40% of gross (to cover labor + takedown + commission)
- **Example:** $1,200 job × 40% = $480 minimum revenue to cover fixed costs. Max discount = $720 (60%).
  - **Realistic safe max:** 20-25% discount to leave room for COGS and profit.

## Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) — Estimated

### Categories (Industry Averages + Assumptions)
1. **LED Lights (C9 or mini lights, per linear foot)**
   - **Estimated cost:** $1.00-$1.50/ft wholesale (bulb + wire/stringer)
   - For 150 ft roofline job: 150 ft × $1.25 = **$187.50**

2. **Clips, hooks, fasteners**
   - **Estimated cost:** $0.10-$0.15/clip (bulk pricing)
   - For 150 ft @ 1 clip/ft: 150 × $0.12 = **$18.00**

3. **Timers, controllers**
   - **Basic timer:** $10-$15 wholesale
   - **Smart controller:** $30-$50 wholesale
   - Average per job: **$15-$25**

4. **Wire, extension cords, connectors**
   - **Per-job consumables:** $10-$20
   - Extension cords, splice connectors, wire nuts, electrical tape
   - Estimate: **$15/job**

5. **Storage materials (bins, labels, bags)**
   - **Amortized over customer lifetime:** $5-$10/job
   - Bins reused, but allocated per job for accounting
   - Estimate: **$7/job**

6. **Vehicle fuel, wear-and-tear**
   - **Mileage-based:** Assume 20 miles round-trip @ $0.65/mile = $13
   - Or flat per job: **$10-$15/job**

## Margin Targets

### Gross Margin Goal
- **Healthy target:** 40-50% gross margin (revenue - COGS - labor)
- **For $1,200 job (updated with COGS estimate):**
  - Revenue: $1,200
  - COGS (21.7%): $260
  - Labor (32.5%): $390
  - Gross Profit: $1,200 - $260 - $390 = **$550** (45.8% gross margin) ✅

### Net Margin (After all costs)
- **After takedown (7.5%) and marketing/flex (5%):**
  - $550 - $90 (takedown) - $60 (marketing) = **$400 net profit**
  - **Net margin: 33.3%** ✅
- **This is strong.** Most service businesses aim for 20-30% net.
- **If marketing/flex isn't spent:** $400 + $60 = $460 (38.3% net margin)

## ROI on Goods (Investment Recovery)

### Leasing Model (You Own the Lights)
- **Business owns lights:** Leased to customer for the season
- **Repairs included:** Any issues = your responsibility (part of service)
- **Buyout option:** Customer can purchase lights after 2+ years
- **ROI model:**
  - Lights are capital assets, amortized over 3-5 years
  - Example: $300 light set used 3 seasons @ $1,200/job = $3,600 revenue → 12x ROI
  - After 3 seasons, offer buyout at $150-$200 (50% residual) and deploy new set
- **Advantage:** Recurring revenue, customer retention (they need you to service), upsell storage
- **Risk:** Upfront capital for inventory, repair costs
